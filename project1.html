Introduction

Yoga is a traditional practice that combines physical postures, breathing, meditation, and mindfulness to improve both body and mind. It increases flexibility, strength, balance, and relaxation.

Benefits

  • Improves flexibility and mobility
  • Builds strength and endurance
  • Improves posture and body awareness
  • Reduces stress and supports better sleep
  • Boosts energy and mental focus

History

Yoga has roots in ancient India and has developed across centuries as a practice for physical, mental, and spiritual well-being. Classical texts such as the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali outline a comprehensive path combining ethics, breath, posture, and meditation.

Yoga stands as one of humanity's oldest philosophical and practical systems, with roots stretching back over 5,000 years into the ancient Indian subcontinent. The word "yoga" derives from the Sanskrit term "yuj," meaning "to yoke," "to unite," or "to join," reflecting its core philosophy of harmonizing the mind, body, and spirit. Vedic Foundations (1500–500 BCE) The earliest references to yoga appear in the Rigveda, the oldest of the Hindu scriptures, dating to approximately 1500 BCE. During this period, yoga was primarily a spiritual discipline associated with ascetics and priests who sought deeper self-awareness through meditation and breathing practices. The Vedas contain references to tapas (inner heat and discipline) and proto-yogic concepts, though formal yoga practices were not yet systematized
